# How Fallout builds change a player's options

Give three players the same destination. What can each do on the way, what does it cost, and which decision still matters when they arrive? The games below answer these questions differently. The proposed design experiments are original hypotheses for Graveforge.

## Fallout 3 — choosing where to invest

The PC manual separates the seven SPECIAL attributes, skills that receive points and perks with their own requirements. Agility contributes to Sneak and the Action Points available to V.A.T.S. Charisma benefits Speech and Barter. V.A.T.S. pauses the game while the player chooses actions from an AP budget. In dialogue, a marked Speech challenge displays a chance of success. Some attribute, skill or perk qualifications instead unlock a line that succeeds when selected. Karma records the character’s overall moral standing. [Bethesda, *Fallout 3* PC manual, “Stats Menu,” “V.A.T.S.” and “Dialogue”](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/22300/manuals/fallout3_us_pc_manual.pdf)

**Design experiment.** At a clockwork fair, let a contestant pause a mechanical exhibition to queue a move, and let a trained adjudicator identify an overlooked scoring rule. Both can win the same entry token. Show the contestant their action budget and show the adjudicator whether their qualification is sufficient. Ask each player what could still go wrong before they commit.

## Fallout: New Vegas — who knows you changes what you can say

The original PC manual describes skill points at each level, a perk every other level, and V.A.T.S. pausing the action to queue commands using AP. Reputation is tracked for individual towns and factions. It can affect help and dialogue, and faction clothing can change an NPC’s reaction. Some dialogue options show the current skill against a required value. A player who sees a shortfall can plan to revisit. The optional Hardcore mode adds needs including food, water and sleep. It is a separate selection from the difficulty setting. [Obsidian and Bethesda, *Fallout: New Vegas* PC manual, “Hardcore Mode,” “Leveling Up,” “V.A.T.S.,” “Reputation” and “Dialogue”](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/22380/manuals/fnv_gfw_manual-10-steam.pdf)

**Design experiment.** Give a surveyor information that helps the ferry cooperative and complicates a market contract. Let the ferry dispatcher open a later passage because the cooperative remembers the favor. Test the same speech skill with each group. Ask the player which outcome depended on their training, which on the favor, and what they could do if a group declined to help.

## Fallout 4 — perk ranks and places worth maintaining

Bethesda folded the earlier standalone skill system into a chart tied to SPECIAL ranks. A new level gives an opportunity to take a perk or improve an attribute. Many perks have further ranks with level requirements. Sneak, for example, is a ranked perk. V.A.T.S. slows the action while the player selects a target, leaving a different time pressure from the full targeting pause described in the two earlier manuals. [Bethesda, “Fallout 4’s Character System”](https://bethesda.net/en-US/news/fallout-4%27s-character-system) · [PlayStation, *Fallout 4* gameplay features](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/fallout-4/)

Companions and competing factions give the journey social stakes. Workshops and settlements give exploration a second purpose. A settlement can serve as a supply stop or a place to upgrade equipment. Bethesda’s revised Survival mode, described in 2016, disables fast travel and ties ordinary saving to sleeping in a bed. Food, water, fatigue and the location of a safe stop can change the value of a detour. These conditions belong to that mode. [Bethesda, *Fallout 4*, “Your Journey Continues”](https://fallout.bethesda.net/en/games/fallout-4/your-journey-continues) · [Bethesda, “Fallout 4’s All New Survival Mode”](https://bethesda.net/en-US/news/fallout-4%27s-all-new-survival-mode)

**Design experiment.** A workshop apprentice has one spool of copper. Spending it now repairs the route beacon. Carrying it home completes a bench that will repair several beacons later. Place a safe rest point on the slower route. Show travel time, remaining stock and what the future upgrade enables before asking which choice felt worthwhile.

## Make each playstyle change a decision

At this original mechanical fair, each row gives a design hypothesis to test in play.

| Playstyle | What the player actually does | Cost or information worth exposing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combat | Enters a staged bout against a spring-driven prize construct and schedules a response to its announced game phase. | A short route spends charge. Preview the cost and whether a turn remains. |
| Stealth | Reads the clock on a theatrical spotlight and takes a backstage route without interrupting the show. | Waiting preserves stock but uses a time slot. Show when the next opening returns. |
| Social | Invokes a guild promise to share a reserved lift. | A favor helps with one guild and might remain unknown to another. Show who granted access. |
| Exploration | Reads an old survey to locate an alternate span. | A detour consumes time and can replenish supplies. Give the landmark a recognizable silhouette. |
| Crafting | Uses a component to repair the sorting gate. | The component is also needed at the destination. Preview the remaining inventory. |
| Survival | Carries provisions or visits a rest stop before the long crossing. | Reaching the finish requires enough stock at every leg. An eventual reward cannot pay an earlier cost. |
| Hybrid | Uses survey training to find a spare coupling and workshop training to fit it. | Splitting points gives up a top-rank action. Remove each training investment in turn and see what actually breaks. |

The **fallout-playstyles** exercise tests whether three builds can complete the same assignment by different means. Predict elapsed time, remaining supplies and standing. Check the routes a player could discover outside the three walkthroughs. In a playable build, also check whether players can recognize an opportunity, understand a refusal and recover. Those observations require gameplay evidence beyond a route simulation.
